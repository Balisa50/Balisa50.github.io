This is the only project on this site that someone else asked for and paid for, and it is here for that reason rather than because it is technically interesting.

A property firm in The Gambia had no web presence and a set of listings that changed weekly. The default outcome for a business in that position is a site built by a developer who then owns every future change, which means listings go stale within two months and the site becomes a liability that outlived its usefulness.

So the deliverable was never the public site. It was the dashboard behind it.

## Why these calls and not the obvious ones

<TradeOffs>

<TradeOff chose="a small admin dashboard" over="a faster static site I would maintain">

Hardcoding the listings would have shipped in a weekend, loaded faster, and cost nothing to host. It also puts me between the client and their own inventory forever, and turns every price change into a message on a Saturday.

The dashboard cost most of the build time and it is the entire value of the project. A site whose content only I can change is a site that is accurate on the day it launches, and never again.

</TradeOff>

<TradeOff chose="admin-only authentication with no public accounts" over="a general user system">

There is nothing for a visitor to log in to. No saved searches, no favourites, no enquiry history. Adding accounts would have meant password resets, email delivery, session handling and a table of personal data to protect, in exchange for features nobody asked for.

One role, one login, no registration route at all. The smallest auth surface that does the job is also the one with the fewest ways to go wrong on a site nobody is paid to monitor.

</TradeOff>

<TradeOff chose="their real listings during development" over="placeholder content">

Building against fake data hides the problems that actually break a launch: property names longer than the layout expects, photographs shot in portrait on a phone, descriptions that run to four hundred words or to nine.

Every one of those surfaced during the build rather than during handover, which is the cheapest time to find them.

</TradeOff>

</TradeOffs>

<Note>

There is no engineering memo below this one, because there is no research phase to write up and inventing one would be dishonest. It is a well-understood application built carefully for someone who needed it, and the interesting decisions were about ownership rather than architecture.

The thing I would do differently is image handling. Photographs go in at whatever size the phone produced, and on the connections most of their buyers are using, that is the slowest part of the site by a wide margin. Resizing on upload is the obvious fix and it is not built.

</Note>
