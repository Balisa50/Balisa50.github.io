title: AYAT
lede: All 6,236 verses of the Qur'an as a point cloud that rearranges itself around your question. Two thirds of the feature I designed did not survive contact with the data, and the version that shipped is better for it.
updated: 25 August 2026
---

Every Qur'an app is a list. Scroll, tap, scroll, tap, 6,236 numbered cells in a column. But the book is not a column. It loops, it cross-references itself, imagery in one surah answers a structure in another. Reading it linearly is reading a hypertext document with the links stripped out.

AYAT is an attempt to make the geometry visible. Every verse is embedded, projected into three dimensions, and rendered as a star. Ask a question and the corpus physically reorganises around it. Ask something it has nothing for and it stays dark and says so.

There is no inference server anywhere in this. That is the reason it can run forever on nothing.

## Why these calls and not the obvious ones

<TradeOffs>

<TradeOff chose="ship the full 384-dimension embeddings" over="reusing the PCA-64 vectors already in the file">

The galaxy already shipped a 64-dimension vector per verse for the layout, so reusing it for search was free. I assumed it would work. It ranked fine and it was badly wrong in a way ranking cannot show you.

Projecting into a 64-dimension subspace and renormalising inflates whatever component survived, which destroys the calibration of the absolute similarity score. Across 34 probe queries, top-10 mean cosine separates on-topic from off-topic with AUC 1.000 in the full space and 0.480 in PCA-64, which is worse than a coin flip. The absolute score is not a detail here. It is the thing that lets the app say the Qur'an does not address a question, and that is the feature I care most about.

Quantising to int8 gets the full vectors down to 2.4 MB, which is a bandwidth cost I will pay.

</TradeOff>

<TradeOff chose="one axis of meaning" over="the labelled ring of arcs I had already designed and published">

The plan was that a question would fan its matching verses into a ring of labelled arcs, so "mercy" would separate into mercy as forgiveness, mercy as provision, mercy as withheld punishment. I built it on synthetic vectors and it was beautiful.

Then I measured it on the actual corpus. After removing the query direction, the residual is essentially one-dimensional: across five queries the first eigenvalue carries 22 to 33 percent of the variance and the second carries 4 to 6, indistinguishable from the third and fourth. My second axis was noise. I tried clustering the matched set instead and k-means silhouette came back at 0.03 to 0.07 for every k from two to six, meaning the groups overlap almost entirely.

The corpus is semantically continuous, not clumpy. So the galaxy spreads answers along one axis that is real and reads as a genuine polarity, from divine attribute to mercy enacted, and the two ends are named by a single cached model call. The visualisation now claims exactly as much structure as the data contains.

</TradeOff>

<TradeOff chose="letting an unanswerable question return nothing" over="always returning a ranked list">

Every search interface returns something. Type anything into any Qur'an app and you get a confident ranked list, which quietly implies that an answer exists and this is it. That is the most common way software misleads people about scripture, and it is entirely accidental.

Because the full-dimension scores are absolutely calibrated, the floor has a measured basis rather than a guessed one: 20 deliberately off-topic queries score 0.187 to 0.442 on their best match, 14 on-topic ones score 0.412 to 0.735. Below the floor the galaxy stays dark and the readout says the Qur'an does not frame it that way. Refusing had to be built on purpose. Nothing about the default behaviour of a similarity search gets you there.

</TradeOff>

<TradeOff chose="steering the projection geometrically from the query vector" over="the published user-steerable projection method">

The academic approach zero-shot-classifies every item with a language model and then re-runs the projection. Reported throughput is around 2.4 to 3.6 items per second, which for 6,236 verses is roughly half an hour per question.

Steering the layout from the query vector alone runs in 22 milliseconds in the browser and spends exactly one model call afterwards, on labelling the two ends of the axis. It is a weaker method that is four orders of magnitude faster, and at this corpus size the difference between the two is not visible to a reader.

</TradeOff>

<TradeOff chose="one THREE.Points cloud with an injected shader" over="InstancedMesh, the usual answer for batching">

Instanced geometry carries a full transform matrix per instance. These particles never rotate and always face the camera, so that matrix is a per-particle cost for something the app does not use. A single Points cloud draws all 6,236 in one call with three floats of position each.

The price was learning GLSL. Getting per-particle glow and pulse out of a Points cloud means injecting a custom vertex and fragment shader through onBeforeCompile with attribute-driven colour and size, which took two weeks I had not budgeted. My first prototype used ordinary meshes in a loop and ran at 4 fps on a phone.

</TradeOff>

## What broke

<PostMortem
  title="Normalising a score to its own range destroyed the ability to say 'no'"
  symptom="A query about Kubernetes pod autoscaling pulled 1,416 verses into the bright core. 'Mercy' pulled 510. The galaxy was most confident about the question it should have rejected outright, and it looked entirely plausible on screen."
  cause="The layout min-max normalised relevance across whatever the current query returned. That stretches any query to fill the full range, so a question with no good matches gets its best-of-a-bad-lot rescaled to look like a strong match. I made the identical mistake in a second place, mean-centring the coherence readout, where it made a deliberately unanswerable question score highest of the four I tested."
  fix="Score against fixed absolute thresholds derived from the probe set rather than against the current result distribution. The floor is now a constant with a measured basis, not a property of the query."
  cost="Made twice, in two different features, in the same project. The second one had shipped."
  lesson="Relative normalisation quietly destroys any claim about absence, and absence is the thing this app exists to express. Any time a score is rescaled to its observed range, the statement 'there is nothing here' becomes unrepresentable."
/>

There was a quieter one underneath it. Relevance was stored as float32 while the bounds were captured from the float64 accumulator, so a value that rounded above the maximum pushed the normalised term just past 1, and a negative base raised to a fractional power is NaN. It affected exactly the single best-matching verse for a query, which is the one verse a reader is most likely to open, and everything else on screen rendered perfectly.

<Note>

I proposed the labelled-arc feature publicly before testing whether the structure existed. The write-up was confident and the demo was persuasive, and the whole thing rested on synthetic vectors with the clusters planted in them by construction. An afternoon of eigenvalue checking would have got me to the one-dimensional version by design instead of by demolition.

Separately, "clustered with HDBSCAN" sat in the README for months while being effectively untrue. UMAP was correctly using cosine distance while HDBSCAN ran euclidean on the raw 384-dimension vectors, where density estimation falls apart. It returned 78 percent noise and one surviving cluster of 33 verses, and I never checked the label distribution because I had validated the visualisation by looking at it.

</Note>
